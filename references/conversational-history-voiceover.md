# Conversational History Voiceover

Use this profile when a long historical script is accurate but sounds like a textbook, an encyclopedia entry, or a formal documentary transcript.

## Target effect

The listener should feel that a well-informed person is telling them what happened and why it became irreversible. The narrator may speak directly to the viewer, pause over a contradiction, translate an old quotation, and make a restrained judgment. Accuracy remains unchanged; only the route into the information becomes more human.

If the user cites a popular-history writer or living creator, extract only high-level mechanisms: short sentences, human motives, causal suspense, plain explanations, dry contrast, and occasional modern analogy. Do not reproduce signature phrases, recurring slogans, distinctive metaphors, or recognizable passage structure.

## Narrative unit

Prefer this spoken sequence:

`concrete action → what the person currently knows → what they fear or want → viewer's likely question → minimum historical context → new evidence or choice → plain-language judgment → pull into the next problem`

Lead with people and decisions. Introduce an office, institution, law, or chronology when the current action makes it necessary. Do not front-load background simply because it comes earlier in time.

## Opening contract

Before the first chapter, use a compact four-stage opening:

1. **Introduce the subject through a specific hook.** Name the person or event and give a comprehensible role, action or evidenced change. The first breath should help an unfamiliar listener understand the subject; do not assume everyone knows the name. Do not read a publication title as a separate opening sentence or insert a channel greeting by default. Follow an explicit user choice or retained project lock when applicable.
2. **Explain the person or event.** Make the role, relationship or change in the hook clear in plain language: the defining action or key change experienced by the subject, and what kind of event this was. Explain a place or term in the event name when needed for understanding, without an etymology or geography lecture. An isolated abnormal result or an intriguing document is not a substitute for this explanation. For a disputed account, qualify the familiar event summary instead of withholding it; a disaster or unresolved event does not require an unproven human actor or cause.
3. **Set the minimum scene before any cultural reference.** Add only the time, active relationship, or prior event needed to understand the conflict. A listener who has seen no relevant drama must already know what is happening. Do not begin with a film, drama, novel, actor, or episode title unless the deliverable is itself a review.
4. **Open one concrete audio hook.** Point to a person, object, message, decision, number, or disputed fact and ask the exact question the next segment will start answering. The first payoff should begin within roughly 30–60 seconds. Avoid empty hooks such as “真相究竟是什么” when the viewer has not been told what is strange.

The opening may reveal the known outcome. It should withhold only the causal explanation that the following scene earns. End the hook on a noun or action the next paragraph can pick up directly.

## Spoken language

- Use everyday Mandarin and contractions natural to speech: “这事儿”“说白了”“问题来了”“你看”“先别急”“到这一步”“换句话说”.
- Use these as attention controls, not decoration. Usually no more than one obvious spoken reset in a paragraph.
- Keep one main idea per sentence. Mix short impact lines with medium explanatory lines; avoid a continuous chain of equally formal sentences.
- Translate titles and ancient quotations immediately. State what the sentence means in the current conflict, not only its literal meaning.
- Prefer concrete verbs over abstract nouns. “没人敢把不同消息送进去” is better than “信息纠错机制失效”. The abstract term may follow after the concrete explanation.
- A mild joke may expose a contradiction, incentive, or bureaucratic absurdity. Never joke about torture, victims, death, disability, ethnicity, or suffering.
- Use direct questions when they express a real causal gap. Do not stack rhetorical questions or ask questions whose answer was already stated.

## Familiar works as bridges

Well-known films, television dramas, novels, or school texts may be used sparingly to help viewers recognize a relationship or decision.

When the user wants a screen-memory-versus-history contrast, or the opening may depend on one, read [screen-memory-contrast.md](screen-memory-contrast.md) before drafting. It owns verification, evidence tagging, opening selection, and copyright boundaries.

- Use only a few bridges when each clarifies a distinct point. Do not add them in proportion to duration or force a quota; the script may be any natural length within its one-hour ceiling.
- Use one sentence to name the verified familiar pattern, then return to the historical evidence.
- Make clear that the reference is an analogy, not a source or proof.
- Prefer broadly recognizable references with a direct connection, such as a school text that already introduces the same historical figure.
- Do not depend on a long quotation, reproduce dialogue, summarize copyrighted plots at length, or force a trending meme into a tragic scene.
- Never import a drama's invented character psychology into the historical record.

## Rewrite method

### Keep research complete and narration selective

Preserve source bindings and uncertainty in the tagged master and research records. In the spoken text, identify a source when quoting it, opening a sustained account, or switching to a materially different account. Repeatedly naming the same book does not make the account more accurate. Keep allegations and self-defence attached to their speakers through the action itself.

For each evidence comparison or limitation, identify the specific judgment it changes, decide whether to keep, consolidate, or move it to the research notes, and show where narration returns to an action or answer. Repeated warnings that sources are not eyewitness transcripts, descriptions of the search process, and source genealogy belong in research notes unless they change the listener's present judgment. Never remove necessary attribution or uncertainty just to reduce citations. Verified useful screen contrasts still need their brief spoken use and payoff.

Review the whole sequence, not a citation count: can the listener follow the people, choices, and consequences without repeatedly being pulled into a methods lesson? A user-requested historiographical explainer can sustain more comparison when that is the actual subject. Do not impose a universal quota or ratio.

For each existing paragraph:

1. Preserve its F/Q/I/D class, source IDs, uncertainty, names, dates, and material facts.
2. Identify the sentence that matters most to the viewer now.
3. Move that consequence, motive, or contradiction to the front when doing so does not distort chronology.
4. Replace formal connectors such as “更重要的是”“这说明”“对本片而言能够确认的是” with a natural transition specific to the event.
5. Turn long institutional explanation into a concrete example, then name the system-level interpretation.
6. End on a changed judgment, a decision, or the next unresolved problem—not a generic summary.

Do not add unrecorded dialogue, interior monologue, gestures, weather, or certainty. Phrases such as “他大概会想” are still invented psychology unless clearly marked as an inference supported by the evidence.

## Density and quality checks

- The opening reaches the anomaly within two sentences.
- The first sentence names the episode's subject, and the next sentence makes the title understandable rather than merely repeating it.
- Any familiar-work bridge appears only after the listener can explain the basic event without it.
- A first-time viewer can paraphrase the event using only the opening: the defining action or key change and what the event name refers to. Check this separately from whether the next segment starts paying the audio hook; do not use later information to repair the test.
- A viewer can follow the story without knowing the dynasty's offices in advance.
- Proper nouns enter only when they become active in the scene.
- Avoid detached glossary paragraphs. A compact explanation of the episode's event name is necessary when it lets a new listener understand the event; a disaster or unresolved event need not be assigned an unproven human cause.
- Spoken resets vary and are not repeated mechanically.
- Familiar-work references are sparse, accurate, short, and explicitly analogical.
- The script remains original and does not imitate a named creator's distinctive voice.
- Removing all filler particles would not collapse the conversational quality; the structure itself must feel spoken.
